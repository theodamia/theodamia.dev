## Honest gaps against the brief

- **A:** The body still has ring joints, visible seams and shell-like shin forms that can read as plates; wear and tiny meadow marks remain busier than the requested few rivets and large smooth forms. Some steel highlights and grass are more yellow/olive than the strict cool palette. The head pennant and antenna remain larger/longer than “small” and “short”. The hut helps suggest scale, but perspective does not prove a literal height multiple. The eyes appear slightly oval in perspective. Its rounded mechanical vocabulary is familiar; an original-design prompt does not guarantee that nobody will perceive a resemblance to existing robots.
- **B:** Some left-hand facets retain pale snow/blue fill rather than being entirely empty air, and the wireframe is slightly denser than “fine and few”. The line colour appears brighter/lighter orange in places than a literal #CF4318 swatch. The right-hand rock and cloud detail is finer than the ideal big simple shapes. This is a concept painting, not a measured colour match.
- **C:** The route is much subtler and sits on the painted slopes, but it begins beyond the hand with a visible gap, rather than literally emerging from the palm. It contains more triangular facets than the two or three requested in the refinement. At 500 px, its faintness weakens the draft/decision distinction, and the physical ridge pennant is very small. The robot retains the mechanical seams, yellowish weathering and plate-like lower legs noted for A. Its standing torso/hip proportions are a variation of A rather than an exact model match. Meadow flower marks and mountain detail are busier than requested.
- **Across the set:** Top-right daylight and lower-left cool shadows are broadly present, but exact hex colours and complete absence of warm reflected light are not enforced pixel by pixel. All are calm, opaque, edge-to-edge Alpine paintings; no visible text, people, animals, magenta, neon or weapons were found on visual inspection. These are selected best tries within the generation limit, not final production paintings.
