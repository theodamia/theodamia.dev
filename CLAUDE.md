# CLAUDE.md

This file provides guidance to Claude Code (claude.ai/code) when working with code in this repository.

## Project Overview

Personal portfolio website for Theodore Damianidis (theodamia.dev). "The Ascent": ten years of software engineering drawn as one long climb. Three routes. `/` is the hero plus the climb: a sticky, scroll-driven mountain scene with one card per job, and a summit that is deliberately never reached. `/cv` is the same ten years read plainly — a field log of every job newest first, then contact — for whoever wants the facts rather than the walk, while the year turns behind it in the valley at the foot of the climb's mountain. `/about` is the summit's page, "what's ahead": it opens on the sky at the visitor's time of day, reads plainly (About, How I work, Skills), and ends at Contact on the next range across a sea of clouds, where the sun comes up as the last screen scrolls in. A floating icon dock at the bottom is the only navigation and works across all three; it ends with the day/night switch.

The visual language is "Alpine": a cool, white, precise interface (slate ink, hairline borders, one orange accent) sitting on a green, sunny, illustrated mountain. By night ("Alpine night") the same tokens turn to dark slate and pale ink, and the mountain is moonlit under stars. Green belongs to the scene, never to the interface. The frozen design reference lives in Claude Design (`The Ascent v4`); changes now happen in code.

## Architecture

- **Framework**: Next.js 16 App Router, React 19, TypeScript (strict mode)
- **Styling**: Tailwind CSS 4 with `cva` for variants. All design tokens live in `@theme` in `app/globals.css` — colours, the two font families, radii, shadows, easing, keyframes. The `@theme` values are the day; the night overrides the same tokens under `:root[data-theme='dark']`, so components never carry two sets of colours. `dark:` is a custom variant on `data-theme` (not the system setting), for the few element-level changes. `touch:` and `touch-xs:` are custom variants for screens that cannot hover (`touch-xs` is below 400px, where the dock drops Home to fit the day/night switch). The one page breakpoint is the `--breakpoint-wide: 900px` token: `wide:` and `max-wide:` in markup, `WIDE_QUERY` in `constants/` for the same line in JS. A `--shadow-*` value is inlined into its utility by Tailwind, so the night's shadows flip through a `--shadow-cast-*` colour instead
- **Type**: Bricolage Grotesque (display; loaded variable with its `opsz` axis, the big headings need the narrow display cut) and Instrument Sans (text), via `next/font/google` in `app/layout.tsx`
- **Scene**: pure SVG and CSS, no WebGL and no chart or animation library. `lib/scene/` generates the mountain deterministically; `components/scene/ascent-stage.tsx` renders and moves it
- **Testing**: Vitest + React Testing Library + jsdom
- **Linting**: `eslint-config-next` (Next, React, React Hooks v7 with the compiler rules) plus the full `jsx-a11y` recommended set as errors and `typescript-eslint`'s type-aware rules on TS/TSX, which is what catches a promise nobody awaits. `eslint.config.mjs` adds rules to the plugins Next already registers, never the plugins' own flat configs
- **Pre-commit**: Husky runs lint-staged (ESLint fix + Prettier on TS/TSX/JS/JSX, Prettier on JSON/MD/CSS)

### The scene boundary

Everything outside `components/scene/` knows the mountain through two things only, so the stage can change (or be replaced) without touching cards, dock or altimeter. The illustrated SVG look is the chosen one: a 3D low-poly renderer was tried and rejected, so "more alive" means small touches on this scene, not a new art style:

- `scene/world.ts` — the mountain as numbers: world size, camp positions derived from `JOBS[].level`, the trail legs as Béziers, and 48 points sampled along each leg **mathematically** (no `getPointAtLength`, so it runs on the server and in jsdom)
- `SceneHandle` (`applyFrame(frame)`, `resize()`) — the stage implements it; `hooks/use-climb-scroll.ts` is the only caller

### Directory Layout

- **A folder is named for what its contents are _about_.** `content/` is the CV and every word on the site; `scene/` is the mountain as numbers; `components/`, `hooks/`, `app/` are what they say. `lib/` is the narrow one: **where our code meets someone else's** — `page-metadata.ts` (Next's Metadata API) and `theme.ts` (view transitions, storage, the pre-paint script). `utils/` is the deliberate exception to the naming rule: generic, domain-free helpers, whatever they happen to import — `cn()` wraps two packages and still belongs there, because you look for it under "how do I merge class names", not under "what does it depend on". Sorting by dependency instead would put `cn` next to the metadata adapter and the mountain maths next to a string trimmer, which helps nobody find anything
- `app/` - `layout.tsx` (fonts, metadata, the dock), `page.tsx` (`/`), `cv/page.tsx` (`/cv`), `about/page.tsx` (`/about`), `globals.css`, `opengraph-image.tsx` (the share card)
- `components/` - One file per piece: `climb.tsx` (the main page's client shell), `trail-link.tsx` (every link between the pages: it moves up or down the mountain), `field-log.tsx` and `log-entry.tsx` (the log on `/cv`: the rail, and one entry on it), `hero`, `job-card`, `altimeter`, `scroll-cue`, `dock`, the pieces of `/about` (`about-hero`, `about-section`, `opinion-list`, `week-bar`, `skill-columns`, `about-finale`, `contact-signal`: the easter egg's trigger), and what pages share (`section-card`, `contact-rows`, `copy-email-button`, `social-links`)
- `components/scene/` - `summit-label.tsx` (the words over the summit: a small SVG of its own in the moving layer, not part of a full-height one, so it can fade when the closing card comes up without repainting the mountain), `ascent-stage.tsx` (the moving stage; it carries `.scene-stage`), `camp-mark.tsx` (one camp: tent, flag, name), `year-backdrop.tsx` (the year behind `/cv` on wide screens), `year-fade.tsx`, `year-weather.tsx` and `year-ready.tsx` (its turn, its weather, its loading), `sky.tsx` (the sky's gradients, the stars, the clouds and `Celestial`, the sun that becomes the moon), `about-sky.tsx` and `about-dawn.tsx` (`/about`'s clouds and its ending: the range, the sun, the saucer), `village.tsx` (the houses at the trailhead), `light-source.tsx` (the lit layer shared by camps and houses)
- `components/icons/` - Inline SVG icons. The dock uses `lucide-react` (Tent, Map, NotebookPen, RadioTower) at stroke 1.75, except Skills: Lucide has no ice axe, so `ice-axe-icon.tsx` is hand-drawn on `dock-icon.tsx` to match Lucide's 24 grid, round caps and joins, `currentColor`. `brand-icon.tsx` holds the three Simple Icons glyphs, and its `BrandIcon` also draws the skills' logos, whose paths come from the `simple-icons` package (server-only, so it never reaches the client). A skill with no logo (a practice) gets a Lucide symbol instead; the pairing lives in `content/skill-groups.ts` (`brand` or `icon` per skill), and both render in `accent-text`, the one touch of colour in the section
- `components/ui/` - Small primitives: `button` and `text` (`Heading`, `Eyebrow`, `Lede`) carry `cva` variants
- `components/__tests__/`, `content/__tests__/`, `scene/__tests__/`, `utils/__tests__/` - Tests colocated in `__tests__` directories
- `hooks/` - `use-climb-scroll.ts` (scroll controller), `use-reduced-motion.ts`, `use-theme.ts` (the theme on `<html>`, and following the system until someone picks)
- `constants/index.ts` - **Only what two or more modules share, or what must match `globals.css`**: the camp anchors, the dock's icon stroke, the three media queries, the section scroll offset and the wide breakpoint. A value a single module uses lives with that module — `STAR_FIELD` in `scene/stars.ts`, `LEG_WEIGHT` in `scene/world.ts`, `THEME_REVEAL` in `lib/theme.ts` — because a number kept far from the arithmetic that reads it is a number nobody can check, and because this file otherwise becomes a drawer
- `utils/` - Our own helpers, one per file, named for what it does — don't add a catch-all `utils.ts`. `cn.ts` (clsx + tailwind-merge), `time-at-progress.ts` (inverse of a cubic-bezier easing), `bare-url.ts` (a URL as a CV prints it), `climb-stops.ts` (where each stop sits on the page — out of the scroll hook so the one piece of arithmetic the whole climb rests on can be tested on its own)
- `lib/page-metadata.ts` - One page's metadata, share card included. **Next resolves `openGraph` per segment and does not merge it field by field**: a page that writes its own replaces the root's whole object and silently takes the generated card with it, so it would be shared as the home page, or as a bare text card. Every page below the root goes through `pageMetadata()` for that reason. `metadataBase` (from `SITE.url`) is what makes the relative canonicals and the card URL resolve
- `app/opengraph-image.tsx` - The card a recruiter sees when the link is pasted into LinkedIn or Slack: the same four facts as the hero, over the same dawn and hills, generated at build so it can never drift from `lib/site.ts`. Satori is not a browser (no `oklch`, no CSS variables, no `clip-path`, and every box with more than one child needs an explicit `display: flex`), hence the plain hex and the hills as one `<svg>`. Its two fonts are committed under `lib/og/fonts` (OFL) so a build never reaches the network
- **The printed sheet.** `/cv` is the page that gets saved as a PDF, so it prints as a CV, not as a screenshot of a website: `print:` variants drop the dock, the year behind the log, the rail, the entry not written yet and the trail's foot, the icon-only social tiles and the closing Contact card, and in their place the header prints the three channels as text (`utils/bare-url.ts`) and the degree prints under the jobs (`EDUCATION` in `lib/about.ts`, which stays off the screen page). The `@media print` block in `globals.css` sets the page margin, forces the day tokens (browsers keep text colour and drop backgrounds, so a night page would print pale grey on white) and prints every absolute link's address after it
- `lib/theme.ts` - Day and night: the no-flash `<head>` script, reading and storing the choice, and `switchTheme()`, the reveal
- **The climb and the plain list.** The same jobs are told twice: `/` walks them oldest first up the mountain, `/cv` reads them newest first as a field log. They are told in two forms: the climb's cards (`job-card.tsx`) and the log's entries (`log-entry.tsx`: open text on a hairline rail, no card, "Mar 2026 – present · 7 months"). Each page owns the `<li>` around its job, because the climb sizes its stops by tenure (`LEG_SCROLL_LVH`) and the log names a view timeline on each (the year behind it, below). `/cv` opens on the facts a CV is scanned for, then the log, on the page itself; on wide screens the page fades out into the landscape beside it, never under the text. On phones it is just the journal. It also carries the print styles, since it is the page that gets saved as a PDF. The dock sends Experience there; the climb links to it from its closing card and from the hero, one quiet dotted line under the social tiles. That line is a signpost, not an apology — it names what is behind it ("Every role, on one page") rather than offering an alternative to a climb the reader has not seen yet, which is what made an earlier version of it read as the hero excusing itself. Plain words on purpose: the reader it is there for is skimming, and a line that leans on the mountain metaphor asks them to decode it first. The recruiter with ninety seconds should not have to find that door behind an unlabelled icon in the dock
- `content/` - `jobs.ts`, `skill-groups.ts`, `about.ts`, `site.ts`, `dock-items.ts`. All page content. Nothing is fetched; edit the CV here, not in JSX. Changing a job's `level` moves its camp and the trail around it
- `scene/world.ts`, `scene/scenery.ts`, `scene/stars.ts`, `scene/noise.ts` - Geometry, the deterministic scenery generator, the star field and their noise (integer hashing; no `Math.random`, no trigonometry, so server and browser paint the same markup)
- `test/setup.ts` - Global test setup and mocks (Next.js router, browser APIs)

### Key Conventions

- Path alias: `@/*` maps to project root (e.g., `@/components/dock`, `@/utils/cn`)
- Components use named exports (`export function Component()`), pages use default exports
- Server Components by default; use `'use client'` only when necessary. The two scene components are client components on purpose: the drawing is generated from code on both sides instead of travelling a second time in the RSC payload
- File names: kebab-case. Component names: PascalCase
- Prettier: single quotes, JSX single quotes, semicolons, 2-space indent, 100 char line width, trailing commas (es5)
- Blank lines are the one thing Prettier leaves alone, so ESLint sets them (`@stylistic/padding-line-between-statements`): one before every `return`, one on each side of an `if`/`for`/`while`/`try`/`switch`, and one after the imports and any directive. `eslint --fix` writes them for you
- Use `cn()` for conditional/merged Tailwind classes
- Use `cva` (class-variance-authority) for component variants
- **A branch that does work gets braces.** A one-line `if` is only for a guard that _leaves_ — `return`, `throw`, `continue`, `break` — where the condition and the exit are both in view. `if (cond) doThing();` is not allowed: the side effect hides at the end of a line you were reading for the condition. An `else` always takes braces. Enforced by three `no-restricted-syntax` selectors plus `@stylistic/nonblock-statement-body-position`. **It cannot be the `curly` rule** — `curly` counts statements and measures lines but never looks at what kind of statement the body is, so `"all"` would flag every guard in the codebase and `"multi"`/`"multi-line"`/`"multi-or-nest"` wave `if (x) doThing();` through exactly as readily as `if (x) return;`. Don't swap it in. It covers everything we write, `scripts/` included: the pixel loops there are mostly `continue` guards, which the rule allows, so the build tool is held to the same standard as the app
- **JSDoc every exported function, and say why rather than what.** A `/** */` above the export, in the voice the rest of the file uses: what the thing is for, what a reader would get wrong, what was tried and rejected. There is deliberately no lint rule for it — a linter can only check that a comment exists, which is how you get comments written to satisfy a linter. Skip it where the name genuinely says everything, and never write one that only restates the signature
- Avoid nested ternaries; extract to variables or early returns
- Extract magic numbers into named constants
- Add a token in `@theme` rather than hardcoding a colour in JSX, and give it a night value under `:root[data-theme='dark']` unless it is the same in both (no `white` or `rgb(30 43 55 / …)` literals, except white text on the accent: text on an ink fill is `on-ink`, a shadow or hole is `shade`)
- **The frame rule.** While scrolling, write only `transform` (pixels, snapped to the device pixel grid) and one `opacity`, on whole layers. No CSS variables feeding `calc()`, no layout reads, no SVG attribute changes, no React state per frame. Everything that needs layout is read in `measure()` (resize and `ResizeObserver` only). This is what keeps the climb smooth — keep it intact if you touch `use-climb-scroll.ts` or `ascent-stage.tsx`
- Elements the stage moves per frame get their resting pose from the `transform` property (`[transform:…]`), never from Tailwind's translate utilities: those set the separate `translate` property, which would add to the per-frame transform instead of being replaced by it
- Camps are **not** part of the big SVG layers. Each is a small SVG of its own (`camp-mark.tsx`) in a layer that moves with the mountain, so its arrival animation (`data-reached` flips when `use-climb-scroll` reports a new stop: the flag runs up and the name brightens; rules under `.camp-*` in `globals.css`) repaints only that small element. Anything else that should animate inside the scene follows the same pattern — never animate inside a full-height layer
- Camp artwork is generated (Gemini) from the prompts in `art/camps/README.md` and processed by `node scripts/process-camps.mjs` into `public/camps/camp-<start year>.webp` + `lib/scene/camp-art.json` (keyed by the job's start year, so adding or removing a job never shifts the artwork). `camp-mark.tsx` shows the artwork when the manifest has an entry and a hand-drawn tent otherwise. The name always stays in code. The flag is one generated picture shared by every job camp (`raw/flag` → `flag` in the manifest, pole and stones, `poleX` where the pole stands): its cloth is lifted into a layer of its own (`kind: 'flag'`), which runs up the pole on arrival and then ripples, never restarting (like the spinner); the drawn pole and pennant remain only as a fallback. **A camp never changes colour or size**: arrival is the flag's cloth running up its pole, the name brightening and the camp's one animated item. A camp listed in the script's `LIGHTS` (a list per camp; `kind: 'fire' | 'lantern' | 'spinner'`, seed pixels inside the shape, or `door` for a doorway drawn dark) has those shapes lifted into their own layers (`lights` in the manifest) and is saved with its lights out and its moving parts removed (anything with moving parts is drawn as a picture of its own and placed beside the camp through `ART_EXTRAS` in `camp-layout.ts`, so nothing is ever behind it); on the page the layers are plain HTML elements (`.camp-light-source[data-kind]`) animated with opacity and transform only, so the compositor handles them and nothing repaints. A fire catches and flickers; a lantern fades in and breathes. The village houses at the trailhead (`VILLAGE` in `camp-layout.ts`, `raw/village-<n>`) go through the same pipeline; their lights are `kind: 'window'` and follow the night instead of the climber: no `data-reached`, a `data-wave` target of the theme reveal, dark glass by day and lit (steady, no breathing) as the night reaches them. A house without artwork is still drawn as a flat hut by `scenery.ts`. It stands to the right of where the trail arrives. `lib/scene/camp-layout.ts` holds where artwork stands and how wide each camp's is, and both `camp-mark.tsx` and `scenery.ts` read it: the mountain's level ledge under a camp is sized from that camp's artwork (`campGround`), so a wide camp never hangs over the edge where the ridge starts to fall. How deep a picture is sunk below the ridge is per image (`ART_SINK_SHARES` in `camp-mark.tsx`): side-on art stands on the ridge, art drawn from above sits on the mountain's face
- **The sky's motion and the snow.** The climb has no seasons: one sky, day or night (`/cv`'s year is its own, below). The sky is alive on its own, all compositor work: painted clouds carried left to right by a steady wind and billowing (`CLOUD_WIND` in `components/scene/sky.tsx`), stars that scintillate by night with rays on the brightest (`scene/stars.ts`), two rare shooting stars, and the snow of the heights (`scene/snow.ts`, `components/scene/snow-layer.tsx`), which the camera fades in from the Lead camp (`SNOWLINE_CAMP`) and thickens toward the summit, one opacity per layer per frame. Below the snowline the green meadows get no weather. Keyframes are generated with plain numbers and **long cycles**: the browser wakes the main thread whenever a CSS animation's cycle ends, so short cycles on many elements cost a style recalc nearly every frame. Reduced motion: no snow, no twinkle, the clouds stand still
- **The year behind `/cv`.** One place, a meadow at the edge of a larch wood, while the year turns as you read: the page opens on today's season and every hairline passed turns to the next. The seasons are a mood, never a comment on a role, so no picture sits beside a particular job. `scene/seasons.ts` holds it as numbers: seven states (the header, every job, the foot), each carrying **all four seasons as columns** (picture `j` of state `k` is season `j + k`), so the season the year starts in picks a whole column with one CSS rule. That start is written to `<html>` as `data-year-start` by `SEASON_SCRIPT` in `<head>`, beside the theme's script: the page is built once and served for months, so the date cannot come from the build, and `<html>` outlives client navigation. Each entry's `<li>` (and the foot) names a view timeline (`--year-k`), `timeline-scope` on the page's `<main>` lets the backdrop find them, and each state fades in on its hairline and out on the next: CSS scroll-driven opacity on whole layers, no script. Without scroll timelines the year stays on today's season. On wide screens the backdrop is fixed behind the page and is the climb's own world seen from the valley at its foot: the season's sky, the climb's stars, its sun that becomes the moon (`Celestial`), its painted clouds on its wind (the `cloud-cross` and `cloud-billow` keyframes), its far ranges and summit (the same flats as the climb, as CSS backgrounds; the meadow hides their cloud sea but for a band of mist), then the seasonal meadow repeated across the bottom and the grove at the right, cropped by the screen's top and right edges (on screens under about 1280px it slides right so its young larch stays clear of the sheet). Everything at full strength, as on the climb. The header and the log read on the page itself: a veil of the page colour that fades out just right of the 620px column and eases off over the meadow, so the journal is part of the page and the ground still runs edge to edge. The weather falls only right of the list. One kind of weather per season, so it reads at a glance: sakura petals, dandelion seeds and yellow leaves are painted (a kit in `art/cv/raw/weather-{day,night}.png`, packed by `pnpm seasons` into one atlas per theme; each season uses one kind from its row, in its poses, and each particle is cut from the atlas by its background position and size), and winter's snow is the climb's own soft round flake, in CSS. Its motion is generated like the climb's snow (plain numbers, long cycles): petals and leaves sway and turn about a tilted axis, which flips them edge-on as they fall, and seeds rise and rock under their parachutes. It is drawn in the browser (`year-weather.tsx`), for the one season each state shows: on the server it would carry all four, twice. Phones have no room beside the log, so there is no year on them: the backdrop is `display: none`, which downloads none of its paintings. The pictures are CSS backgrounds on purpose, since a picture not displayed is not downloaded: the later states wait for the page to load (`data-year-ready`), only the theme on screen shows, and a theme seen once stays underneath the other (`data-year-seen`), so the day/night switch shows the old paintings until the new ones arrive, never a meadow with no wood. The paintings are `art/cv/raw/{grove,meadow}-<season>-<theme>.png` (prompts in `art/cv/SEASONS-README.md`), processed by `pnpm seasons` into `public/seasons/` and `scene/seasons.json`: keyed, each night given its day's outline, the magenta the painter reflected into thin tufts and grass cleaned (only the pixels that carry it are repainted: a twig a few pixels thin is all edge, and repainting every edge pixel smears colour along it), the meadow made a seamless tile, and the colour under transparent pixels filled before WebP encodes it (WebP keeps colour at half resolution, and a hidden colour bleeds into thin twigs as fringes). Reduced motion: the year steps at the same hairlines, and no weather
- **`/about`: what's ahead** (`app/about/page.tsx`, `scene/about.ts`, rules under "/about: what's ahead" in `globals.css`). The summit's page looks forward, not back. It opens on the climb's own sky at the visitor's time of day: `DAYPART_SCRIPT` in `<head>` writes `data-daypart` (morning, midday, evening, night) to `<html>`, like the season's script, and the opening's gradient mixes the dawn and day tokens by it (by night those tokens are dusk and midnight), with the climb's clouds on its wind and, by night, its stars; no sun or moon there, the sun is the ending's. The title stands on that sky in the page's ink; everything read is on the page. Then the facts a recruiter scans first and "Email me", still in the first screen; then About, How I work (the opinions as statements with how often they hold in words, `holdsInWords`, and a five-step scale; the week as one proportion bar, the accent on its largest share) and Skills (plain lists, logos beside them, no pills), each section's name in a margin that stays in view. It ends at Contact, one screen and the page's last, nothing pinned: the card near the top, a band of open sky, and the next range along the bottom edge. The range is one painting in three lights with one outline (`range-sunrise`, `range-day`, `range-night`); as the screen scrolls in (a view timeline on the section, `entry` range), dawn becomes morning and the climb's `Celestial` comes up from behind the right-hand ridge (by night, dusk becomes midnight and the moon rises). How tall the range stands is measured on the painting (`SUMMIT`, `CLOUD_LINE` in `scene/about.json`) and, since the range spans the screen, set in `cqw` of the section, so the card never overlaps the summit at any width. The one easter egg: making contact (Copy or "Email me", marked `data-contact-action` in `contact-rows.tsx`) sets `data-contact` on the section (`contact-signal.tsx`), and a small saucer comes in from high in the sky, hovers in the band under the card, blinks its running lights twice (glows at the points the script found on the painting) and swings back up into the sky: eight seconds, once, three wrappers so its path curves; it sits between the sky and the range, so the peaks can hide it. Reduced motion: the sun steps, the saucer only fades in and out. Phones: just the words. The paintings are `art/about/raw/ahead/land-{sunrise,day,night}.png` and the top-left piece of `art/about/raw/ufo-{day,night}.png` (prompts in `art/about/`), processed by `pnpm about` into `public/about/` and `scene/about.json`
- The sun is fixed at the top right, just left of the altimeter's card (hidden on phones, where the massif fills the top of the screen). It does not move with the scroll; only the sky does: dawn to day by day, dusk to midnight by night, from the same two layers and the same one opacity per frame (only their tokens change)
- **Day and night.** `data-theme` on `<html>` is the only switch. The inline script from `lib/theme.ts` sets it in `<head>` before the first paint (stored choice, else the system's; always day where `light-dark()` is missing, and the switch hides there), so there is no flash; `<html>` has `suppressHydrationWarning` for that reason. `color-scheme` follows it. There is no theme library
- **Trees:** the near layer's trees and the four framing pines in the foreground are pictures (`tree-1` spruce, `tree-2` fir; `TREE_PICTURES` in `scenery.ts`, `<image class="scene-tree">` in the layer markup, dimmed at night); far layers keep flat two-triangle silhouettes, which suit the haze. A tree picture never stands on the silhouette edge of a steep flank (it would hang over empty air and read as floating): `planted()` moves it down onto the hill's face, below the ground on both sides of its trunk, so the hill is behind it. The early mountains (wooded near bands up to the Geekbot ledge) also get a scatter of trees down their faces (`faceTreeSpots`, `FACE_TREES`), kept clear of the trail (`clearOfTrail`, drawn over this layer), of every camp's flag, art and name (`clearOfCamps`) and of each other; drawn top to bottom so nearer trees overlap further ones. Trees under the foreground mist look pale on purpose. Every picture in the scene is lit from the left: the fir is always mirrored because it was drawn lit from the right, and nothing is ever flipped at random
- **The scene's colours follow the theme without a second drawing.** `scenery.ts` writes every generated colour as `light-dark(day, night)` (night from `moonlit()`), with the day colour also as the plain attribute for browsers without `light-dark()`; everything else in the scene uses tokens (`ink` for line work, which turns pale at night; `shade` for shadows, holes and doorways, which stays dark; `halo`, `cloud`, `wisp`). Never write a bare colour into scene markup. At night the camp artwork is dimmed with a filter and its light layers are not, so fires and lanterns glow
- **Moving between pages** (`components/trail-link.tsx`, the `<ViewTransition>` around every page in `app/layout.tsx`, rules under "Moving between the pages" in `globals.css`). The three pages are three heights on one mountain: `/cv` the valley, `/` the climb, `/about` the summit. Every link between them is a `TrailLink`: next/link with the navigation run in a transition named for its direction (`addTransitionType`: `trail-up`, `trail-down`). Going up, the page leaves upward and the next rises in from below, as the climb's cards move when you climb; going down, the reverse. The old page leaves in 240ms with a slight haze, and the new one eases in over 560ms on `--ease-soft`, a beat later. Only the pictures move: the two pages' boxes are never morphed into each other. The dock is a `<ViewTransition>` of its own (`dock-still`), or the page's picture would cover it while it moves. React names these boundaries only during its own transitions, so the day/night reveal (a plain `document.startViewTransition`) is untouched. A link to the page you are on, a modified click or another site is left to the link itself. `ViewTransition` and `addTransitionType` come from the React that Next bundles with `experimental.viewTransition` (types: `react-canary.d.ts`). Reduced motion: a plain cross-fade
- **The reveal** (`switchTheme()` in `lib/theme.ts`, rules under "Switching day and night" in `globals.css`): a view transition keeps the old page still and shows the new one through a soft-edged circle that grows from the toggle (`--reveal-r`, animated from script, ease-out, `THEME_REVEAL`). The new view is the live page, so things in the sky change on the wave: any element with `data-wave` gets a `--wave-delay`, when the circle's edge reaches it, and its own CSS transitions wait for it. The sun becomes the moon that way (`.celestial`: the earth's shadow slides over it from the lower left), and each star comes out that way (`.star`, with a lag: the brightest first). Anything new that should change with the night in the sky follows the same pattern. Reduced motion: the browser's own cross-fade, no circle, no wave. No view transitions: an instant switch, with the wave still timing the sky
- Every stop is a job, but the trail starts lower: camps are waypoints, `[TRAILHEAD, ...JOBS]` (`CAMP_COUNT = JOBS.length + 1`). Camp 0 is the trailhead (village, signpost, no card, no altimeter mark, always reached); job `i` stands at camp `i + 1`. Legs are sized by **compressed tenure** (`legWeight(years)` in `lib/scene/world.ts`, limits in `LEG_WEIGHT`): the same weight sets a camp's height above the last one and the `min-height` of that job's `<li>` (`LEG_SCROLL_LVH`), so long stays scroll longer and the camera keeps one speed. It is deliberately not proportional to years (a six-month job would be a sliver no card fits in) and not calendar-based (gaps between jobs never show). Adding or removing a job in `lib/jobs.ts` needs no other change
- The altimeter (`components/altimeter.tsx`) is the trail in miniature, fixed on the right: "Summit" (`SUMMIT_MARK` in `content/site.ts`) at the top, Start at the bottom, and a two-line mark (year and camp `place`) for every stop; the present one says "Now". The rail is a map, so its ends name places. The stance — `SUMMIT_LINE`, "The peak is still ahead" — belongs to the scene's summit label and the top of the log on `/cv` (the entry not written yet), and deliberately **not** to the altimeter: the rail and the summit are both in view at the top of the climb, and a stance read twice at once stops being one. A test pins that the altimeter does not repeat it. The marks are plain right-aligned text with a soft halo (`text-shadow-halo`, the card colour, so it flips with the theme); only the stop you are at is an ink pill (pills of different widths side by side looked uneven). Walked is solid ink and still to climb is dotted, like the scene's trail. Per frame the scroll controller moves only the needle and scales the walked bar (transforms); the lit mark and filled nodes change only when a new stop is reached. On wide screens the list of cards carries `.clear-of-altimeter`, a right padding that keeps right-hand cards clear of the labels at any width; keep it if the labels get wider
- Job cards live in normal flow over the sticky stage (same grid cell), never inside the moving world. Everything is on the card at once, nothing behind a button: role, period and a one-sentence `summary` on top, then a pale "What I did" panel with two or three `highlights`; the company name is the card's only link. A content test keeps the copy short (summary ≤ 150 characters, points ≤ 72). No toggles, overlays, drawers or modals anywhere
- The root must not get `overflow-x: hidden` (it would become a scroll container and break `position: sticky`); `body` uses `overflow-x: clip`
- The dock is icon-only, so names come from `aria-label`; bubbles and icons are `aria-hidden`. Tests query by role and name. The day/night switch sits after a hairline, outside the `Sections` landmark (a setting, not a place); its icon (a moon by day, a sun at night: where the click takes you) is picked by CSS so it is right from the first paint
